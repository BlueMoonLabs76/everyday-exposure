# ADR 0007: The map is vector tiles in one PMTiles file

Date: 2026-09-15
Status: Accepted. Replaces the one self-contained HTML page per build. Revised the same day: roads are drawn by class from zoom 5, and every street from zoom 11, over a smoothed grid. Revised again 2026-09-16: Local Roads cross-fade with the grid instead of swapping, and the map's background is a named neutral land color.

## Context

Until now a build wrote one `heatmap.html` with every street inside it. For Virginia that page was 98 MB, and for California, with 1.07 million Origins, writing it ran Node out of JavaScript heap. The country has about 9 million Origins (ADR 0004), and people look at it through Selections, not one state at a time. The map needs a form that holds the whole country and still lets anyone find their own street.

## Decision

**The map is vector tiles in one PMTiles archive, drawn in the browser by MapLibre** (the user, 2026-09-15). `npm run heatmap -- <state>` saves the state's scores in `out/<state>/results.bin`. `npm run map` draws every built state into `out/map/`, or into another folder with `--out`: `exposure.pmtiles`, the page `index.html`, each Selection's figures in `selections.json`, and a street-name index in `search/`. `npm run serve` serves that folder, because the page reads the archive a piece at a time over HTTP range requests.

- **Written in plain Node.** The tiles (Mapbox Vector Tile 2.1) and the archive (PMTiles v3) are encoded by `src/tiles.js`, with no dependency and no tippecanoe, which is not installed here and has no native Windows build. The page loads MapLibre and the PMTiles reader from jsDelivr.
- **State by state, merged in tile order.** Each state's features are saved as records sorted by tile (`out/<state>/tiles.bin`), and the archive is written by merging every state's records one tile at a time. Memory holds one state at a time, as the scoring does.
- **The further out, the fewer roads** (the user, 2026-09-15: lesser streets and roads disappear as you zoom out, and interstates still show at state zoom). A way is drawn from the zoom its class earns (`ROAD_ZOOM` in `src/map-layers.js`): interstates (`motorway`) from zoom 5, `trunk` from 7, `primary` from 8, `secondary` from 9, `tertiary` from 10, and ramps and every Local Road that gives an Origin from 11. Local Roads that give no Origin, the destinations, and each street's trip join at zoom 12. Below zoom 12 a line is thinned to a pixel and carries only its name and Exposure; a click there zooms in and opens the street's trip. Streets are drawn in their Exposure color, the other roads in gray, darker while zoomed out so interstates read over the grid.
- **A smoothed grid colors the land below zoom 11** (revised after the user found the first grid's tiling poor). From zoom 3 to 10 each tile is a 128 × 128 grid, and each cell is colored by the mean Exposure of the streets within two cells of it, weighted toward its own by a 5 × 5 binomial kernel, on the same color ramp. A cell fades with the weight of streets around it, so a lone rural street is a faint dot and towns are solid, and over the grid each street's line has a light edge. The weighted sums add across states, so merged cells are what one build of the country gives. Mapped ALPR Cameras are drawn from zoom 6, state outlines at every zoom.
- **Local Roads cross-fade with the grid instead of swapping at zoom 11** (revised 2026-09-16, the user: "the streets dont fade to oblivion and somehow merge to the large heatmap tiles"). Before this, a Local Road's line had no tile data below LOCAL_ZOOM (11), so the whole neighborhood's streets popped in at once in the same frame the grid square under them disappeared. A Local Road's line is now also written one zoom early, at LOCAL_ZOOM − 1, tagged `local`; the page fades that copy's opacity from 0 to 1 across zoom 10-11 while fading the grid's opacity from 1 to 0 over the same span, both by a continuous zoom expression, so the swap reads as one thing dissolving into another rather than two things taking turns. Every other road class was already spread across multiple zooms and needed no change.
- **The map's background is a named neutral land color**, `#e1ded2`, distinct from the panel's off-white (the user: "land should be a neutral land color"). It used to be the same tone as the side panel, so the map read as more UI chrome rather than land; it is now visibly its own surface, and it is what shows through wherever the grid has no weight (open water, an unbuilt state, or real rural blank), reading the same everywhere "no data" is true.
- **Rural streets look like every other street** (the user, 2026-09-15). A street's trip minutes, and whether only its first 40 km counted (ADR 0006), show when it is hovered or selected.
- **"Find your street" reads static files.** Street names are grouped per county or independent city and sharded by the first two letters of each word, leaving out street types and directions. There is no geocoder and no outside network call.
- **Selections are the built states, each state, and its counties and independent cities**, from OSM boundaries. Metro areas and ZIP code areas come later, from dated national files (ADR 0004).
- **The disclaimer and the ODbL credit travel with the map.** The page shows the short disclaimer in view and the full text a click away; the corner credit links OSM's copyright page; the archive's metadata carries the attribution; `NOTICE.txt` sits beside the data files.

## Considered options

- **One page per county or independent city, plus an index.** Opens by double-click and reuses the old page, but gives no national view, and about 3,100 pages.
- **One page that loads each state's streets as you pan.** Opens by double-click, but Leaflet cannot draw millions of lines, so zoomed out it would show nothing.
- **tippecanoe.** The usual tool for vector tiles, but not installed and not built for Windows. Its feature dropping is replaced here by drawing each road class from a fixed zoom.
- **The first grid: 64 × 64 cells a tile from zoom 3 to 11, each cell colored by its own streets only, and no roads below zoom 12.** Half the tile bytes, but MapLibre draws a vector tile 512 pixels wide, so a cell was 8 to 16 pixels: blocky squares across a state, and at zooms 10 and 11 scattered specks, since a cell there holds a street's midpoint or nothing. A finer grid alone traded the blocks for speckle.
- **Every street as a line from zoom 10, with nothing but the grid below.** It read well at zoom 10 and 11, but streets appeared all at once and no road showed across a state. Holding Local Roads back to zoom 11 first left flat-colored squares around lone rural streets at zoom 10; fading cells by their weight fixed that.

## Consequences

- **The page can't be opened from disk.** It says so, and `npm run serve` is one command. Publishing it needs a host that answers range requests; which one stays tabled with the other publishing decisions.
- **A state's own page is gone.** `heatmap.html` is no longer written. `exposure.csv` and its notice are unchanged.
- **Zoomed out, the map shows smoothed means, not streets.** A cell's color is the weighted mean Exposure of the streets in and around it, so it can read as 1 where most streets are 0 and a few nearby are high, and color spills a cell or two past the edge of where people live. Where streets are sparse, around zoom 9 and 10, the faded cells make a mottled texture. Its tooltip gives the share that pass no camera and how many streets lie in the cell itself.
- **Tile sizes.** The first state measured had an archive of 36 MB (15 MB with the first grid), its largest tile 243 KB at zoom 7. With every street at zoom 10, California's was about 115 MB with a 1.09 MB tile over Los Angeles; holding Local Roads to zoom 11 should shrink that tile, but California has not been measured again.
- **The zoom 10-11 cross-fade cost that state about a third more.** Writing Local Roads' lines one zoom early raised the archive to 48 MB (from 36 MB) and the largest tile to 595 KB, now at zoom 10 (249/406) instead of zoom 7 — that tile carries a full zoom of Local Road lines it didn't before, on top of the grid. Not re-measured for California.
- **States not yet built are blank.** The page lists the states built so far, and its country figures cover only them.
- **Changing how tiles look doesn't need rescoring.** `npm run map` rebuilds a state's records from `results.bin` and `data/<state>/`, so `data/<state>/` must be kept until the map is built. `RECORDS_VERSION` in `src/map-layers.js` is bumped whenever the records change, so no state's old records are reused.
